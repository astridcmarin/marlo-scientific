Computational pharmacology lab

Build a synthetic patient, then run published models on that record. Results are typical values from a published model, never a prediction for a real person.

  • Lenalidomide concentration curves from two population PK models (Hughes 2019, Kim 2026).
  • How elimination splits between kidney filtration, tubular secretion and other routes.
  • Relative odds of severe low blood counts, and the bortezomib platelet course (Lignet 2022).
  • Building blocks for target binding, protein turnover, delays and dosing schedules.

Provenance first, including where a model falls short

A simulated number is only as useful as what you know about it. Every value records where it came from and what has, and has not, been checked.

See every model's scorecard

  • Pinned sources

    Reference data is checksum-pinned and versioned. A changed file is refused; updates are new versions.

  • Four checks per model

    Code, transcription, reproduction and outside data, each reported separately.

  • Plans fixed in advance

    Comparisons with outside data follow a plan locked before they run, and results are kept either way.

  • Unknown stays unknown

    No silent defaults or unit conversions. Every assumption is named with its status.

Built by Astrid Marin

Marlo Scientific is designed and developed by a pharmaceutical scientist working across regulatory science, clinical pharmacology, and pharmaceutical development. Astrid holds an MSc in Pharmaceutical Sciences (Pharmaceutical Affairs) and a BS in Biomedical Sciences (Biochemistry), and is trained in ICH-GCP and clinical research coordination.

She built Marlo Scientific to help innovate and accelerate drug research and development with AI: bringing computational pharmacology and AI together with the evidence researchers already rely on, so that faster tools never lose sight of where their answers come from.

The platform reflects that background: evidence first, every value traceable to its source, and a clear line between simulation and clinical fact.
