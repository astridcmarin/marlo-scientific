Production and loss

Imagine a generic protein being made continuously and removed at a rate proportional to how much is present. At the starting baseline, production and loss balance, so relative amount is 1.

In this invented experiment, loss is multiplied by 4 from hour 0 to hour 4, then returns to its baseline rate. Production stays constant throughout. The protein amount remains continuous at hour 4; it does not jump back to baseline.

dR/dt = kout × (1 − multiplier × R)

R is an amount divided by an unspecified baseline amount. It is dimensionless, not a binding fraction or an absolute concentration. During a constant positive multiplier m, the equilibrium is 1/m and the relaxation rate is kout × m.

What this adds to the platform

Binding describes interaction with a target. Turnover describes production and removal of a downstream quantity. This demonstration provides turnover mathematics without assuming that target binding controls it.

No binding curve, lenalidomide concentration, IKZF assay or patient record feeds this example. Such connections need a supported mechanism, calibrated parameters and validation data.

Concept reference: Dayneka, Garg and Jusko (1993), indirect pharmacodynamic response models. The reference motivates the model family; all rates and the schedule here are invented.
