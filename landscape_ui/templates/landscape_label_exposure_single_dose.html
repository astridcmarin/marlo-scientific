{{ v.label }}: typical exposure after one dose, from the product label

Typical value from the label — not a population model, not a prediction for any patient

This curve is built from the numbers in the HEMADY (dexamethasone 20 mg tablets) prescribing information. The label reports them for a single dose “in subjects” and does not say whether those subjects had multiple myeloma. There is no variability around the curve (the label reports 31% variation in exposure), and it gives no dosing guidance.

In patients with myeloma, measured exposure was higher. In 6 Japanese patients given 40 mg, the average exposure was about {{ fmt1|format(1 / v.checks.outside.primary.ratio_model_to_observed) }} times this curve and their clearance was {{ fmt1|format(v.checks.outside.mm017_cl_f_l_h) }} L/h against the label's {{ fmt1|format(v.parameters.cl_f_l_h) }} L/h. Six patients cannot settle the difference, but read the curve as a lower-side typical value for patients.

The label's myeloma dosage is {{ v.regimen.quote|replace('The recommended dosage of HEMADY is ', '')|replace('.', '') }} (section 2.1), together with other anti-myeloma drugs. The label states that exposure is proportional to dose from 0.5 to 40 mg, so 40 mg is shown as twice the 20 mg curve.
