Bortezomib and platelets: typical time course

Typical curve from mean data — not a toxicity prediction

This page shows how a published model (Lignet et al. 2022) describes the average fall and recovery of platelet counts on the studied bortezomib schedule. Individual patients vary widely around it, and the model has no information on that spread. It does not predict whether any patient will develop thrombocytopenia and gives no dosing guidance.

Clinical prediction validated: false · Patient simulation ready: false

Schedule

Bortezomib 1.3 mg/m² IV on days 1, 4, 8 and 11 of each 21-day cycle — the regimen the model was fitted to; other doses and schedules are not run.

How the model works

Platelets come from progenitor cells in the bone marrow that divide, mature through three stages (mean maturation time MTT) and are released into the blood. When circulating platelets fall below their starting level, a feedback signal (strength γ) speeds up division — which is why counts recover during the rest week. Bortezomib reduces the process in proportion to its plasma concentration, through the term (1 − slope × C). The authors fitted two versions that differ only in where the drug acts; both reproduce the observed average fall, so both are shown.

Which variant is right is not settled. For bortezomib the budding variant fits the published data better (AIC 2013 vs 2037) and matches laboratory evidence that bortezomib impairs platelet release; but its maturation time, 846 h, is about six times longer than any published platelet value. The myelosuppression variant has plausible parameters but the less likely mechanism. A good fit does not prove a mechanism. Over several cycles the two diverge — one dips deeper each cycle, the other rebounds above baseline — showing how much the unresolved mechanism matters beyond the data it was fitted to.

Above these, (1 − slope × C) is negative and the model briefly removes cells rather than slowing them. The fitted parameters reproduce the data only with the term used this way (locked check), so it is read as an empirical description of the net effect on average counts, not a literal mechanism. Concentrations here come from the paper's PK model, which has one clearance and overstates exposure after the very first dose about 7-fold.
