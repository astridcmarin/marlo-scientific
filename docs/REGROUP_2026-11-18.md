## 1. The six unread sources: none can be a blind check yet

**Where it stands.** A blind check needs a platform model whose prediction is locked before the data are read. None of the six has one:
- Badros 2013 and Quach 2017 are carfilzomib; carfilzomib has an extracted label and no curve.
- Dimopoulos 2021 and the SARCLISA EPAR are isatuximab; label extracted, no curve.
- NCT04256707 is selinexor; label extracted, no curve.
- The Zhang and Mager supplement holds a bortezomib model's parameters, not observations: material to build from, not to check against.

**The path.** The roadmap already queues label-consistency plans for carfilzomib, selinexor and thalidomide after extraction review round 3. A carfilzomib curve (eligible through the intravenous engine) would make Badros and Quach its first blind test. One limit to record in that plan: the KYPROLIS label summarises renal studies that may include these two, so the check is independent of the tabulated values but perhaps not of the label's renal statement.

**Recommendation.** Carfilzomib first, because two unread sources wait for it. Order: Track B closes review round 3 → carfilzomib label-consistency plan → carfilzomib curve → a locked blind check against Badros and Quach's tabulated values.

**Decision:** carfilzomib first, or another drug?

## 2. Eligibility and protocol benchmarking

**Where it stands.** Both extractors failed every criterion type blind. The reference they were scored against is not shown adequate: agreement 0.8925 against a 0.90 bar, and adjudication found the reference wrong in at least 31 of 400 pairs. Round two (guide v3, 50 fresh trials, plan v2.1 locked) waits on the owner and annotator B.

**What round two decides.**
- *Bar met:* the v3 reference is usable. Next is an extractor revision, tested on trials posted after the cohort freeze, never on these 200.
- *Bar missed:* reading eligibility criteria consistently is harder than the guide can fix. Protocol benchmarking then either narrows to the criterion types readers agree on, or leaves the product's first release.

**Recommendation.** Do round two before any extractor work. Decide now what a miss would mean, so the result is read against a rule written beforehand.

**Decision:** if round two misses the bar, narrow benchmarking or drop it from the first release?

## 3. What a user should be able to trust first

**Where it stands.** The positioning is "the evidence reference for people designing and running drug development". The trial explorer, special-population evidence for six drugs, the dose view and guided path for lenalidomide, credibility records and the findings page all exist. The models carry heavy caveats, by design. Phase 7 (going online) and user conversations are not started; user conversations were postponed by the owner.

**Options.**
- **A. Evidence first.** Lead with what is sourced rather than modelled: trial landscape, special-population evidence, regulatory references, findings. Models stay as research tools behind their records.
- **B. Models first.** Keep checking models until some pass cleanly, then lead with them.
- **C. Users first.** Hold five to ten conversations with trial designers, clinical pharmacologists or regulatory professionals before choosing A or B.

**Recommendation.** C, then most likely A. Two months of checks show the evidence layer is the part a user can trust today. Without conversations, every further step is a guess at what is wanted.

**Decision:** A, B or C, and if C, when?
